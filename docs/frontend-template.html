Datasets & coverage

What was evaluated

12 Indic DiarBench recordings (two each in Hindi, Bengali, Marathi, Tamil, Telugu, Kannada); six AMI-SDM meetings in English; six VoxConverse recordings tagged English. Seven manifest languages, not nine. AMI uses whole meetings; other sources use deterministic excerpts up to five minutes.

205 minutes of scored audio, 196 minutes of reference speaker-time. Native reference conventions are preserved. Structural differences cannot establish whether content or annotation policy caused a score difference.

Evaluation contract

Protocol & limitations

A result that can be reproduced

Identical 16 kHz mono input; zero collar; overlapping speech included; no true speaker-count hint. Saved RTTMs are scored with pyannote.metrics. DER includes missed speech, false alarms, and confusion. JER uses speaker-level intersection over union after matching. Global tables use a recording mean, not pooled speaker-time DER.

Low activity: at most 5% of reference speaker-time and at least 0.5 s. Recovered: a one-to-one matched hypothesis covers at least 50% of reference speech. This is a proposed diagnostic; sensitivity results appear in the main report.

Full-sample uncertainty: 10,000 recording-level bootstrap resamples. Pairwise DER tests use Bonferroni correction and a predeclared 1 pp practical threshold. Filtered means here have no recomputed intervals and no inferential rank.

Limitations that affect a purchase decision

  • 24 recordings and two per Indic language; no reliable language-level ranking.
  • Anuvani has Indic training-corpus exposure; its parent also lists AMI/VoxConverse. community-1 has AMI/VoxConverse exposure. Exact session overlap remains unaudited; Nemotron exposure is unknown.
  • Nemotron ran streaming while the other systems ran offline. Its eight-speaker capacity is exceeded by HI-01 (nine) and VC-01 (thirteen); these remain scored.
  • References were audited structurally, not by listening. Code-switch boundaries were not annotated.
  • Timing uses one GPU type and a fixed panel. Callables differ in I/O and post-processing scope; setup/load is separate. RTF does not measure live latency. Immutable checkpoint/environment capture is incomplete.
  • cpWER, tcpWER and WER/CER were not run. The listening room contains three attributed original excerpts; it does not provide transcripts, separated stems, or computed similarity scores.

Governance

Curated submissions require a pinned checkpoint, frozen settings, complete run, exposure statement, and independent reproduction. Bring-your-own results remain self-reported and unranked until reproduced. Reference corrections create a new version and trigger all-system rescoring with old/new/delta publication. Archive prior boards; never silently re-rank.

Reflection / next evaluation

With more time

Add cpWER and tcpWER

cpWER concatenates words by speaker and finds the permutation with the fewest word errors. tcpWER adds a time constraint so implausibly distant words cannot match. Both test speaker-attributed transcription and need recognized words plus human-checked reference text; tcpWER also needs a timing policy.

Hold the ASR backend fixed to compare diarizers. Treat complete vendor ASR+diarization systems as a separate track. Freeze normalization, language-specific tokenization and tcpWER timing tolerance on development data. Use a pinned MeetEval version. None of these scores is claimed as measured here.

Complement the core metrics

Retain DER/JER, speaker-count accuracy/MAE/bias, low-activity recall, overlap-only DER and RTF. Add WER/CER, recognition-versus-attribution diagnostics such as DI-cpWER or ORC-WER, speech-activity and overlap precision/recall/F1, boundary errors, and live finalization latency with their own definitions and eligibility rules.

Broaden the datasets

DIHARD III for diverse acoustic domains; ICSI for meetings; CALLHOME for telephone; DISPLACE for Indian multilingual conversations; AliMeeting for Mandarin near/far-field meetings; CHiME-8 DASR for distant multi-device conversation. Verify licenses, held-out splits, training exposure and reference compatibility before evaluation.

First close the current gaps: matched offline/streaming Nemotron runs, human listening plus adjudication, exact-session exposure checks, and more independent recordings per slice. Then broaden hardware and measure cost per audio-hour.

A speech evaluation lab, built in controlled stages

Speaker separation: compare original mixtures with recovered stems on isolated-source references, using permutation-aligned SI-SDR and downstream DER/tcpWER plus listening checks. Speaker similarity: evaluate held-out same/different-speaker pairs, EER/minDCF and calibrated false-accept/miss trade-offs; cosine similarity is not an identity probability.

Clustering: hold segmentation/embeddings fixed, inspect merges/splits and count errors, and report adjusted Rand and homogeneity/completeness measures with DER/JER. VAD/SAD: measure speech/non-speech precision, recall, F1 and time-based misses/false alarms under a shared speech definition.

Use immutable versions, disjoint test identities/conversations, controlled ablations, tracking, review and repeatability. These tracks are proposed. The listening room currently plays the original mixture and saved turns; no separated stems, similarity scores or new detector outputs have been computed.
